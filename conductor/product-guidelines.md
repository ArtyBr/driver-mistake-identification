# Product Guidelines

## Tone and Voice
- **Direct & Safety-Critical:** The application communicates with a serious, professional tone suitable for safety training. Feedback is objective and factual, avoiding ambiguity.
- **Urgent Priority:** Safety violations are highlighted with urgency to underscore the potential consequences of the error (e.g., using terms like "Critical" or "Unsafe" rather than "Suggestion").
- **Clear & Concise:** Explanations for errors are brief and to the point, focusing on the specific mechanics of the mistake (e.g., "Lane departure without mirror check") to ensure the user instantly understands the issue.

## User Experience (UX) Principles
- **Clarity of Cause and Effect:** The UI must visually link the road event (e.g., car moving left) with the driver's action (e.g., head still facing forward) in the same view, ensuring the user understands *why* an event was flagged.
- **Efficient Review:** Users should not have to watch hours of safe driving. The system prioritizes "Safety Events," allowing users to jump directly to the moments that matter.
- **Visual Confirmation:** Use overlays (bounding boxes, gaze vectors, lane lines) as the primary means of communication, letting the visual data tell the story rather than relying solely on text logs.

## visual Identity
- **High Contrast:** Use strong contrast colors (Red/Green/Yellow) for status indicators to ensure immediate readability.
- **Clean Dashboard:** The interface should be uncluttered, focusing entirely on the video player and the event timeline.
- **Data-Rich Overlays:** Visual elements like lane lines and head pose indicators should be distinct and not obstruct the critical view of the road or driver.
